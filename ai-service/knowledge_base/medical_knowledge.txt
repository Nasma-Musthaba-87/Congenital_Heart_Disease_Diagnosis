Congenital Heart Disease Knowledge Base

Congenital heart disease refers to structural abnormalities
of the heart that are present at birth.

Atrial Septal Defect (ASD):
Atrial septal defect is a condition characterized by an
opening in the wall between the two upper chambers
of the heart.

Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD):
Ventricular septal defect is an opening in the wall
between the two lower chambers of the heart.

Patent Ductus Arteriosus (PDA):
Patent ductus arteriosus occurs when a fetal blood vessel
between the aorta and pulmonary artery remains open
after birth.

Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF):
Tetralogy of Fallot is a congenital heart condition
involving four structural abnormalities of the heart.

Clinical evaluation of congenital heart disease may
involve echocardiography, electrocardiography,
physical examination, and patient history.

Echocardiography provides information about the
structure and function of the heart.

Electrocardiography records electrical activity
of the heart and may provide information about
heart rhythm and electrical conduction.

Medical imaging and physiological signals should
be interpreted by qualified healthcare professionals.

Artificial intelligence systems in healthcare require
appropriate training, validation, and clinical evaluation
before clinical use.
